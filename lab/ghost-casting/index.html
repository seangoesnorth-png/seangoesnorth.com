Casting

A photoreal campaign shoot with no model, no camera, no location. We ran a casting session, locked an identity, shot a forty-frame campaign, and then taught a neural network her face. Every person on this page is invented.

FIG I / LAB FILE / 07.31.26 TALENT: 0 HUMANS PIPELINE: CAST → PLATE → TURNAROUND → TRAIN
FIG II / THE CASTING

Six candidates. None of them exist.

Spec work, running-spot energy. The brief called for a fit Asian American woman, a runner, with one identity anchor: a small beauty mark under her left eye. Six candidate plates were generated as a real casting session — same setup, six different faces — and reviewed like any callback sheet. Candidate 04 got the part. We call her Mei.

THE CASTING SHEET · SIX INVENTED CANDIDATES · 04 BOOKED THE JOB

Every single candidate rendered the beauty mark on the wrong side. Two different AI systems, ten generations, one lesson: prompts propose, the approved plate decides. That is production discipline, not prompt engineering.

FIG III / THE TURNAROUND

Same woman. Every angle.

A talent asset is useless if her face changes between takes. The approved plate became the single source of truth, and a six-angle studio turnaround was generated against it — front, three-quarters, profiles, back — the same reference sheet a character department would build for a CG double.

MEI · STUDIO TURNAROUND · ONE IDENTITY, SIX ANGLES

Then we did it again for a second character — a basketball spot, a different invented athlete, his own identity anchor (a scar through his left eyebrow). The system is repeatable. That is the point.

DANTE · CHARACTER 002 · SAME SYSTEM, NEW SPOT
FIG IV / THE SHOOT

Forty frames. No call sheet.

With the identity locked, a local render rig shot the campaign overnight: ten setups in running-spot grammar, four takes each. Pre-dawn stretch, launch stride, rain grind, golden-hour recovery, the hero stare-down. Face locked to the plate on every frame. The GPU never asked for catering.

FIG V / THE TRAINING

Watch a machine learn a face.

The final step: a 26-image dataset built from the plate and turnaround, and roughly four and a half hours of training on a desk GPU. The strip below is the same prompt rendered at checkpoints during training — from the model’s generic guess at step zero to Mei, locked, at step 2500.

ONE PROMPT, SIX CHECKPOINTS · STEP 0 → STEP 2500

The result is a reusable identity: one trigger word now produces Mei in any scene, any light, any lens — studio portrait or full sprint at golden hour — with no reference image attached. She is directable talent.

The tools are interchangeable. The roles are not. This page was produced by agentic employees in studio roles — casting, continuity, QC, training — choosing whichever tool won each stage.
