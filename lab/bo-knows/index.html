FIG II / WHY THIS EXISTS

The film is the resume. The pipeline is the argument.

I have spent 20 years executive producing for Nike, Jordan, the NFL, and Spotify. Productions with trucks, crews, casting directors, and catering budgets. This piece was produced by one person at a desk.

The honest version of how it got made is not “I typed a prompt.” It is the same discipline as any production I have ever run: boards first, a locked design language, and a consistency system enforced shot by shot — because generative models will happily redesign your main character between takes if nobody is watching.

FIG III / THE PIPELINE

Four stages. One design language.

01 / BOARDS

Script & structure

Six chapters, locked before a single frame generated. VO written and cut to time. The edit exists on paper first.

02 / PLATES

Midjourney

Hundreds of generations curated down to hero plates. Low-poly geometric figures, 1988 palette, weathered screen-print texture. Rejects outnumber keepers 10 to 1.

03 / MOTION

Kling

Plates animated with tightly constrained motion prompts. Every clip carries a must-not-change list: face, palette, light direction, silhouette.

04 / FINISH

After Effects

Conform, titles in TS Block, captions, sound design, grade. The stage where it stops being clips and becomes a film.

FIG IV / THE DESIGN LANGUAGE

200 generations that read as one film.

The style is the consistency system: faceless low-poly geometry, a restricted 1988 palette, chunky distressed type with partial-letter cropping. Constraints are what make volume generation direct-able — every frame either belongs to this world or gets cut.

What the tools replaced. What they didn’t.

Replaced

  • The location, the truck, the schedule

Not replaced

  • Taste — knowing when a frame is wrong
  • Story structure and the edit
  • Direction: constraints, consistency, intent
  • The 10,000 hours that trained the eye

That second column is the job. It has always been the job.
